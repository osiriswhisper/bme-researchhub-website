### A novel mRNA Lipid Nanoparticle Therapy Improves Heart Failure Phenotype and Suppresses Endothelial‑Mesenchymal Transition In Vitro

**Core engineering problem**  
Designing a safe, efficient, and transient delivery system that can translate exogenous BMP‑7 mRNA into functional protein within cardiac tissue, thereby attenuating fibrosis without off‑target or toxic effects.

**Methodology & tech stack**  
- *LNP formulation*: A patent‑pending, custom‑design BMP‑7 mRNA encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (AET‑1978) optimized for subcutaneous administration.  
- *In vivo model*: Non‑ischemic heart failure induced in mice via L‑NAME and angiotensin II; therapeutic dosing of AET‑1978; assessment through serial echocardiography (LVEF, diastolic parameters) and histological fibrosis quantification (Masson’s trichrome).  
- *In vitro EndMT assay*: Human umbilical vascular endothelial cells (HUVECs) exposed to pro‑EndMT stimuli (TGF‑β, hypoxia) and treated with BMP‑7 mRNA‑LNP; evaluation of morphology, mesenchymal marker expression (α‑SMA, N‑cadherin), endothelial function (NO production, LDL uptake, tube‑formation).  
- *Safety profiling*: Serum markers for renal/hepatic injury and histopathology of major organs.

**Biomedical innovation**  
AET‑1978 offers a non‑integrating, transient mRNA‑based therapeutic that restores BMP‑7 levels locally, reduces myocardial fibrosis, improves both systolic and diastolic function, and preserves endothelial integrity. This strategy could translate into a novel pharmacologic intervention for chronic heart failure, complementing neurohormonal blockade and addressing a key mechanistic driver—fibrosis—via a scalable, lipid‑nanoparticle platform.
