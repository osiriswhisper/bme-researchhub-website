### Nanostructured Zirconia thin films as neurogliomorphic interface for neural cells of central and peripheral nervous system

**Core engineering problem:**  
Developing a biomimetic, nanoscale interface that can reliably interact with heterogeneous neuroglial networks, enhancing glial calcium signaling while maintaining cell viability, for future brain‑machine and neuromorphic bioelectronic devices.

**Methodology & tech stack:**  
- Fabrication of nanostructured cluster‑assembled ZrO₂ (ns‑ZrOx) thin films versus flat zirconia controls.  
- Primary astrocyte cultures and dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neuron‑glia co‑cultures seeded on the substrates.  
- Live‑cell calcium imaging (Fluo‑4/AM) to quantify transient amplitude and kinetics.  
- Immunocytochemistry for adhesion (β‑actin), differentiation markers (GFAP, β‑III‑tubulin), and viability assays.  
- Electrical characterization of memristive behavior using current‑voltage sweeps to link material properties with biological response.  

**Biomedical innovation:**  
The study demonstrates that ns‑ZrOx surfaces actively modulate neuron‑glia communication through enhanced calcium signaling, positioning these nanomaterials as *neurogliomorphic* interfaces. This paves the way for adaptive, bio‑integrated neural implants and neuromorphic computing platforms that can interface seamlessly with living neural networks, potentially improving the performance of brain‑machine interfaces and other neuroprosthetic devices.
