### Genetically engineered rapamycin responsive K2P channels

**Core engineering problem:**  
Designing a modular, high‑affinity, and subtype‑selective method to activate endogenous K2P potassium channels with a small, drug‑like molecule (rapamycin or its analogs), enabling precise chemogenetic control of membrane potential.

**Methodology & tech stack:**  
- Genetic fusion of the FRB domain of mTOR into a flexible cytoplasmic loop of TREK‑1 (K2P channel).  
- Co‑expression or endogenous recruitment of FKBP partners to form an FRB/rapamycin/FKBP ternary complex.  
- Electrophysiology (single‑channel recordings) to quantify changes in open probability and unitary current.  
- Cryo‑EM structural determination to capture the activated channel conformation.  
- Extension of the strategy to other K2P isoforms, demonstrating versatility.

**Biomedical innovation:**  
This work creates a new chemogenetic toolkit that allows researchers to switch on specific potassium channels with nanomolar rapamycin concentrations. In neural systems, such precise control of K2P activity can modulate neuronal excitability, offering potential applications in neurotherapeutics, in vivo electrophysiology, and the development of dynamic neural interfaces that respond to pharmacological triggers.
