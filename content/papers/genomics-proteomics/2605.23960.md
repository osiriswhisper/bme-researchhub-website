### Learning Protein Structure-Function Relationships through Knowledge-guided Representation Decomposition

**Core engineering problem**  
Deep learning models for proteins generate high‑dimensional, highly entangled embeddings that obscure the biophysical signals governing function. The challenge is to disentangle these representations into biologically meaningful, task‑relevant dimensions while preserving predictive power.

**Methodology & tech stack**  
- **ProtDiS framework**: Applies a knowledge‑guided decomposition of pretrained micro‑environment embeddings (e.g., from AlphaFold‑style models) into independent, interpretable sub‑spaces.  
- **Information bottleneck principle**: Balances informativeness and compression, encouraging sparsity and independence among learned features.  
- **Training pipeline**: Fine‑tunes on 12 downstream tasks (e.g., secondary structure, binding site prediction, functional annotation) using PyTorch; employs contrastive loss and mutual‑information regularization.  
- **Evaluation**: Structure‑based cross‑validation, residue‑level attention maps, and fold‑function discrimination analyses.  
- **Open‑source implementation**: `https://github.com/AI-HPC-Research-Team/ProtDiS`.

**Biomedical innovation**  
By producing concise, interpretable protein embeddings, ProtDiS enables more reliable functional annotation and structure‑based drug target identification. This supports accelerated protein‑druggable‑target discovery, facilitates precision medicine through better understanding of mutation effects, and can be integrated into bioinformatics pipelines for vaccine design and enzyme engineering.
