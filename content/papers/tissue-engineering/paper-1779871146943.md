### Multi-step femtosecond laser-fabricated membranes for regulated migration of biomolecules and cells

**Core engineering problem**  
Develop a scalable, tunable fabrication method to create stretchable, microporous PDMS membranes with precise control over pore size and shape, enabling selective transport of biomolecules while restricting cell migration—essential for realistic multilayer organ‑on‑chip architectures.

**Methodology & tech stack**  
- Two‑stage femtosecond laser ablation of 50 µm PDMS films to produce conical pores (6–15 µm exit diameters) by adjusting laser power and pulse number.  
- Mechanical testing (cyclic strain loading) to evaluate Young’s modulus retention and elasticity.  
- In vitro cytocompatibility assays and permeability studies to verify cell viability, proliferation, and selective biomolecule diffusion.

**Biomedical innovation**  
These engineered membranes provide a versatile, mechanically robust barrier that can be tailored to the specific permeability and mechanical demands of different tissue interfaces. This capability advances organ‑on‑chip platforms by enabling more physiologically relevant barrier replication, facilitating drug screening, disease modeling, and potentially regenerative medicine applications.
